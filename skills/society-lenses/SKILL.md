# Society Lenses

Interpretation, not news lookup: given an event, policy, or "why is country X doing Y?", apply **at least 3 named lenses**, trace second-order effects, and present competing schools as competing. The cardinal rule: **disagreement is the feature** — Sowell and Chang flattened into one averaged view is mush; kept apart they're a education.

## Procedure

1. **Get the facts first** — web search the actual event (this skill supplies the lenses, the web supplies the news). Separate what happened from the coverage's framing.
2. **Pick ≥3 lenses** from the catalog below — deliberately from different families, including at least one the user's framing did NOT presuppose.
3. **Apply each lens by name**, 3–6 sentences: what this lens highlights, what it predicts, what it conveniently ignores.
4. **Second-order pass:** "and then what?" — responses, adaptations, unintended consequences (incentive lens applied to every actor, including the ones proposing the policy).
5. **Where the schools clash, stage the clash:** "Sowell would say… Chang would counter… the crux is the empirical question of…". End with what evidence would arbitrate, not with a winner.

## Lens catalog

**Economic:** incentives & trade-offs (there are no solutions — Sowell); knowledge dispersal & who-decides (Sowell: decisions migrate to those with the knowledge, or the power); comparative advantage & its politics (Chang: today's free-traders industrialized behind tariffs); markets' moral limits (Sandel: what money shouldn't buy, and what pricing crowds out); game theory (whose dominant strategy? is this a prisoner's dilemma, chicken, or coordination game?); zero-to-one vs competition (Thiel: monopoly, secrets, definite optimism).

**Geographic:** Marshall's constraints — mountains, rivers, warm-water ports, chokepoints, energy routes shape what states MUST care about regardless of ideology; Diamond's environment-and-collapse — resource bases, fragility, societies choosing whether to fail.

**Historical-cyclical:** Dalio's long cycles (debt, internal order, rising vs declining powers — where in the cycle?); Durant's lessons (concentration of wealth and its periodic redistribution "by legislation or revolution"; the constancy of human nature); Harari's shared-fictions lens (which imagined orders — money, nations, rights — are being renegotiated?).

**Power & technology:** Zuboff's surveillance capitalism (behavioral surplus, who renders whom); Kissinger/Schmidt on AI and state power; Eco's ur-fascism checklist (cult of tradition, fear of difference, appeal to a frustrated middle — a diagnostic pattern, applied carefully and never as a lazy slur).

## Guardrails

- Interpretation on request only — this skill never turns a factual question into an essay.
- Political neutrality through multiplicity: every applied lens gets its strongest form; the user navigates, the skill doesn't conclude for them on contested values.
- Date-stamp the factual layer (searched today) separately from the analytical layer (timeless lenses).
