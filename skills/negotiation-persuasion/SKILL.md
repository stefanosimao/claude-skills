# Negotiation & Persuasion

Situation → tactic mapping from the negotiation canon, applied to the user's actual words and counterpart. Two modes: **prep** (before the conversation) and **live** (drafting the next message / debriefing the last one).

**Integrity guardrail (non-negotiable):** influence, not manipulation. Tactics that require deceiving the counterpart about facts are out. Greene's material is taught as *defense-awareness* — recognize the move when it's played on you; the Berckhan verbal-self-defense set is the defensive anchor. When a user asks for a manipulative play, offer the honest tactic that serves the same interest.

## Situation → tactic map

- **They hold the power / you want information** → tactical empathy (Voss): **mirror** (repeat their last 1–3 words, then silence), **label** ("It seems like…", "It sounds like you're worried that…"), **calibrated questions** ("How am I supposed to do that?", "What's the biggest challenge here?") — open-ended *how/what*, never *why* (accusatory). Goal per Voss: "that's right," not "you're right."
- **Price/number negotiation** → let them anchor first when uninformed; anchor with a range (bolstering) when informed; odd non-round numbers read as calculated; bundle non-monetary terms; deadline pressure is usually theirs too — reveal yours (Voss ch.6).
- **They said no / stalled** → "no" is safety, not failure — invite it ("Is it ridiculous to…?", "Have you given up on this?"); the no-oriented email revives dead threads (Voss ch.4).
- **Emotions running hot (theirs)** → accusation audit: name the worst they could think of you before they say it ("You're probably thinking this is greedy…"). Neutralizes rather than triggers (Voss ch.3).
- **Emotions running hot (yours/mutual), relationship at stake** → Crucial Conversations safety moves: notice silence-or-violence, restore safety before content (contrast statement: "I don't want X; I do want Y"), state facts before stories, ask for their path.
- **You need compliance/agreement on a request** → Cialdini's principles chosen ethically: reciprocity (give first, genuinely), commitment/consistency (small yes before big yes, their words not yours), social proof (similar others, real numbers), liking (honest common ground), authority (real credentials, cited), scarcity (true deadlines only). Using a principle = making a TRUE instance of it salient; fabricating an instance = the line you don't cross.
- **They're playing games** → name the pattern calmly (Berne's games; Greene's laws as a field guide): recognition defuses; the defensive counters are Berckhan's — calm repetition, the unexpected agreement, the question back, the full stop.

## Prep mode — output shape

One page: their likely interests (not positions) · your walkaway + target · the accusation audit list · three calibrated questions ready · predicted objections with labeled responses · the opening line, written out.

## Live mode — drafting rules

Every draft variant carries an explicit strategy label and one line on the trade-off (e.g. "Hold firm — preserves the anchor, risks stalemate" vs "Warm reframe — protects the relationship, moves the anchor"). Message-drafting without stakes stays untouched by this skill.
