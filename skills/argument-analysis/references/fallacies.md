# Working Fallacy Taxonomy

The ~25 that cover 95% of real arguments, grouped by MECHANISM. Format: name — how the move fails — detection cue. For the full ~79-entry catalogue with per-fallacy tactics, fetch `critical-thinking-logic/_summaries/how-to-win-every-argument.md`.

## Misrepresenting the opponent
- **Straw man** — attacks a weaker substitute of the stated position. Cue: the rebuttal answers something not quite said.
- **Hollow man** — attacks a position nobody holds. Cue: "some people say…" with no named holder.

## Irrelevant appeals (red herrings)
- **Ad hominem** (abusive / circumstantial / tu quoque) — attacks the arguer, not the argument. Cue: biography where evidence should be. Note: credibility IS relevant to testimony, not to logic.
- **Appeal to authority** — authority outside their competence, or against consensus of peers. Cue: "Nobel laureate says" on a non-field claim.
- **Appeal to popularity / tradition / novelty** — many/old/new ≠ true. Cue: counting heads or calendars.
- **Appeal to emotion / fear / pity** — feeling substitutes for support. Cue: vivid scenario, no mechanism.
- **Genetic fallacy** — origin discredits content. Cue: "that idea comes from X, therefore…"

## Causal errors
- **Post hoc** — after ≠ because. Cue: sequence offered as mechanism.
- **Correlation ≠ causation** (cum hoc; confounders; reverse causation). Cue: two curves, one story.
- **Slippery slope** — chain of maybes sold as inevitability. Cue: no probabilities on the links.
- **Single cause** — complex outcome, one villain. Cue: "the real reason is…"

## Evidence distortions
- **Cherry-picking / silent evidence** — the graveyard uncounted. Cue: only survivors cited.
- **Anecdotal** — story beats statistics. Cue: "my uncle…"
- **Hasty generalization** — tiny/biased sample, big claim. Cue: n unstated.
- **Texas sharpshooter** — pattern drawn after the data. Cue: hypothesis born from the same data that confirms it.
- **Base-rate neglect** — vivid likelihood beats prior frequency. Cue: no denominator.

## Structural / semantic
- **Begging the question (circularity)** — conclusion smuggled into a premise. Cue: restated claim as its own support.
- **Equivocation** — a key term shifts meaning mid-argument. Cue: the argument works only if the word means two things.
- **False dilemma** — two options where more exist. Cue: "either…or" on a spectrum.
- **Composition / division** — parts→whole or whole→parts illegitimately. Cue: scale jump.
- **Loaded question** — assumption embedded in the asking. Cue: any answer concedes it.
- **Moving the goalposts / special pleading** — criteria change when met; exemption without principle. Cue: "yes, but that doesn't count."
- **No true Scotsman** — redefinition to dodge the counterexample. Cue: "no REAL X would…"
- **Burden shifting / appeal to ignorance** — unproven-false ≠ true. Cue: "you can't prove it isn't."
- **Middle ground** — truth assumed halfway. Cue: compromise offered as evidence.
